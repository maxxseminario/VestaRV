\paragraph{Rules for a record.} Seven, each a measured cost rather than a
precaution. Keep both excitation codes inside one octave: a pair straddling a
major carry is out by \SI{1.5}{\percent} of commanded amplitude, and by
\SI{3.5}{\percent} at the \SI{4}{\milli\volt} excitation, statically and at
every frequency. Use the two-level square where the record is dense (it
delivers \(1.28\times\) the fundamental of a synthesised sine for the same
interface excursion and measures the same impedance to five significant figures),
and a sine where it is sparse, since at the \num{32} conversions per channel
per period a shared converter delivers, the square costs \SI{2.99}{\degree} of
phase and the sine costs nothing. Start the record only after the electrode's
own dc transient has decayed: the excess falls as \(\exp(-t/\tau)\) with
\(\tau = C_{\mathrm{dl}}(R_u \parallel R_{\mathrm{ct}})\) and is a function of
\(t/\tau\) alone, so wait \(5\tau\) for \SI{0.1}{\percent},
\SIrange{99}{9900}{\micro\second} across the three electrodes, and do not
substitute averaging, which attenuates the term only as \(1/T_{\mathrm{win}}\)
and leaves \SI{1.2}{\percent} after sixteen cycles. Derive the converter strobe
and the excitation from one divider, a grid \num{0.35} samples per period out of
step costing \SI{0.6}{\percent} on a reactive load while reading a resistor
correctly, and average with a boxcar rather than by discarding samples, because
a reactive load's \(n\)-th current harmonic is \(Z(f)/(n\,Z(nf))\) of the
fundamental rather than \(1/n\) and the high orders fold into the fundamental
bin. Measure the feedback resistance on every chip and never compute it. And
rotate the phase by \(180\,f/f_s\) whenever one converter reads both channels.
