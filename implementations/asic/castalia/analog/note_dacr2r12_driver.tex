\paragraph{The bit driver is a 2.5\,V cell, and the tables above are measured on
it.} The gate on each code bit was a 1.0\,V standard cell sitting on the
\SI{2.5}{\volt} analog rail, which is a reliability defect independent of
linearity; it was replaced on 2026-09-05 by a custom cell on the 2.5\,V device
family. \textbf{The driver's on-resistance is a linearity term, not a timing
term}: its pull-up and pull-down differ, and at the most significant carry that
difference appears divided by a \(2R\) leg and multiplied by \num{2048}. For the
cell removed, that product is \SI{0.495}{LSB} against the \SI{0.511}{LSB} of
differential nonlinearity it actually produced, which is the whole of the carry
error.

\textbf{The replacement is better than the cell it replaces on both sides.} Over
the same five corners, the same bench and the same tolerance, integral
nonlinearity at the worst corner falls from \SI{0.425}{LSB} to \SI{0.414}{LSB}
and the most negative differential step from \SI{-0.535}{LSB} to
\SI{-0.479}{LSB}, still at code \num{2048} and still monotonic at every point.
The Monte Carlo of
Section~\ref{sss:dacr2r12-mc} moves the same way, to \SI{42.0}{\percent}
monotonic and \SI{20.5}{\percent} within one LSB of integral nonlinearity
against \SI{40.0}{\percent} and \SI{21.0}{\percent}. \textbf{The conclusion of
Section~\ref{sss:dacr2r12-mc} is unchanged: what limits this converter is ladder
matching, and no driver fixes it.}
