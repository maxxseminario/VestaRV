\subsubsection{The amplifier}

In unity feedback into \SI{5}{\pico\farad} the amplifier is comfortable:
\SIrange{84.1}{85.8}{\decibel} of DC gain, a \SIrange{6.2}{7.6}{\mega\hertz} unity-gain
frequency and \SI{88}{\degree} of phase margin at every corner. It is overcompensated
for this load, which costs bandwidth and buys a clean step response.

The asymmetry of the output stage is what carries forward. Sourcing collapses at
\SIrange{13.6}{15.6}{\micro\ampere} --- the fixed current in M5 --- while sinking is not
resolved by the \SI{40}{\micro\ampere} sweep, and the same ratio appears large-signal as
a falling slew rate \num{1.7} times the rising one. At maximum gain this does not bite:
full scale through \SI{560}{\kilo\ohm} is \SI{\pm 2.23}{\micro\ampere}, a factor of six
inside the limit. At \emph{minimum} gain it does. The \SI{\pm 33}{\micro\ampere} target
at $R_f = \SI{35}{\kilo\ohm}$ needs \SI{33}{\micro\ampere} in both directions, more than
twice the sourcing limit, so the half of that range in which the output must source
current through $R_f$ is not deliverable. This is an open item --- a larger M5, a
different output stage, or less full-scale current at low gain --- and it has not been
confirmed in the transimpedance bench, because no low-gain setting was simulated.
