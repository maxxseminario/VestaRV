\subsubsection{Transfer Function}\label{sss:adc-transfer}

The converter is monotonic across the whole sweep, reading code 0 at
\SI{0}{\volt} and code 1023 at \SI{2.5}{\volt} (Figure~\ref{fig:adc-transfer}).
Codes climb 1.535 times faster than ideal: a least-squares fit to the 24
unclipped points of a \SI{0.0625}{\volt} sweep over \SIrange{0.5}{2.0}{\volt} gives \num{628.64} codes per
volt against the ideal \num{409.60}, so the effective full-scale input span is
\SI{1.627}{\volt} rather than the \SI{2.5}{\volt} supply span, and the effective
LSB is \SI{1.591}{\milli\volt} rather than \SI{2.441}{\milli\volt}
(Table~\ref{tab:adc-transfer}). Extrapolating that fit puts code 0 at
\SI{0.441}{\volt} and code 1023 at \SI{2.068}{\volt}, a window centred on
\SI{1.255}{\volt} --- the \SI{1.25}{\volt} channel common mode. The converter
clips outside that window, losing \SI{34.9}{\percent} of the intended input
range, split between the two ends rather than concentrated at one: a
full-scale transimpedance swing about $V_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is truncated
symmetrically.

Top-plate parasitic capacitance is the leading explanation. In a
charge-redistribution SAR the array's own parasitic divides the feedback DAC
swing by $C_{\mathrm{array}}/(C_{\mathrm{array}} + C_{\mathrm{par}})$, which
raises the codes-per-volt slope by the reciprocal of that factor and shrinks the
input span in the same proportion; a ratio of 1.535 implies $C_{\mathrm{par}}
\approx 0.535\,C_{\mathrm{array}}$. It is consistent with the measured window
being centred on the common mode instead of offset from it, and with the effect
appearing only in the extracted netlist. It has not been confirmed by inspecting
the top-plate node, and no other mechanism has been excluded.

Departure from the fitted line is at most \num{0.52}~LSB over the fitted range
(Figure~\ref{fig:adc-error}). That is a residual of a 24-point fit at
\num{39} codes per step, not a linearity figure.

Referred to its own range instead of the nominal one the picture separates
(Table~\ref{tab:adc-transfer}). The usable window is \SIrange{0.441}{2.068}{\volt},
\SI{1.627}{\volt} wide against the \SI{2.5}{\volt} span and centred on the
common mode (Figure~\ref{fig:adc-range}); \SI{34.9}{\percent} of the nominal
range is unreachable, split \SI{0.441}{\volt} low and \SI{0.432}{\volt} high.
Across that window the effective LSB is \SI{1.591}{\milli\volt} rather than
\SI{2.441}{\milli\volt}, and each code sits within \num{0.523}~LSB of the
measured line, \num{0.294}~LSB rms (Figure~\ref{fig:adc-inl}). The two defects
are therefore separable: the range is short by a third, and within what remains
the transfer is straight to better than half a code at the sampled inputs.
