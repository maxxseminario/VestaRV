\subsection{SAR Analog-to-Digital Converter}\label{ss:adc}

\texttt{anatop\_pixel\_adc} is a 10-bit single-ended charge-redistribution SAR
converter (Figure~\ref{fig:adc-sar}). One instance per channel digitises the
transimpedance output of Section~\ref{ss:pixel}: $v_{\mathrm{out}} =
V_{\mathrm{CM}} + i_{\mathrm{we}}\cdot R_f$ enters the converter and leaves as a
10-bit code, offset-binary about the code that corresponds to
$V_{\mathrm{CM}}$. The capacitor array is both the sampling network and the
feedback DAC; its layout is Figure~\ref{fig:adc-capdac}.

Conversion is externally clocked: the macro advances one bit trial per falling
edge of a trigger clock. Every result in this section uses a
\SI{20}{\mega\hertz} trigger; the design-rate protocol takes 23 ticks with a
\SI{400}{\nano\second} sample phase, \SI{1.150}{\micro\second} per
conversion, \SI{869.6}{kSa/s}. The bench steps
the input from \SIrange{0}{2.5}{\volt} in \SI{0.25}{\volt} steps and reads the
code at the settled point of the first completed conversion, where the
converter's ready output is high at every step.

\paragraph{Conversion rate on the chip.} The AFE peripheral derives the trigger
from MCLK, \(f_{\mathrm{trig}} = f_{\mathrm{MCLK}}/2(\texttt{AFECLKDIV}+1)\), and
emits ten full pulses per conversion. At the \SI{24}{\mega\hertz} characterised
MCLK maximum (Section~\ref{s:clocktree}) and \texttt{AFECLKDIV}~=~0 the trigger
is \SI{12}{\mega\hertz}: \SI{1.917}{\micro\second} per conversion,
\SI{521.7}{kSa/s}, at \texttt{AFESAMPLESTEP}~=~7, and
\SI{1.667}{\micro\second}, \SI{600.0}{kSa/s}, at \texttt{AFESAMPLESTEP}~=~4,
whose \SI{417}{\nano\second} sample phase still exceeds the characterised
\SI{400}{\nano\second}. The \SI{869.6}{kSa/s} design rate needs a
\SI{40}{\mega\hertz} MCLK, outside the characterised range. The
\SI{12}{\mega\hertz} trigger is not simulated on the extracted macro: its
\SI{41.7}{\nano\second} bit-trial pulses are longer than the characterised
\SI{25}{\nano\second}, but no transfer, timing or power figure in this section
is taken at that rate.

\paragraph{Rev-1 bench stimulus.} The rev-1 clock stimulus, generated by the
Myshkin converter peripheral that Castalia does not instantiate, holds the
convert phase of each \SI{1150}{\nano\second} pulse group (clear, sample,
convert) statically high: one falling edge where the ten bit trials need ten.
The trials consume the falling edges of the following three groups, and ready
asserts every \SI{4.600}{\micro\second}, \SI{217.4}{kSa/s}. Ten
\SI{20}{\mega\hertz} pulses in the same \SI{500}{\nano\second} window close
each conversion in \SI{1150}{\nano\second} with \emph{identical codes} at every
input level. The \SI{217.4}{kSa/s} figures of Section~\ref{sss:adc-power} are
therefore a property of that stimulus, not of the chip, and are reported only as
a second clock protocol on the same converter.

\paragraph{Coverage.} All data in this section comes from a \emph{post-extraction}
netlist (Quantus, C-only coupled parasitic extraction of the ADC macro)
and is, with the code-level impedance record of Section~\ref{ss:eis} that
splices this same macro into the channel, the only extracted data in this
chapter; every
other section is
schematic-level. The distinction is not incidental: the capacitor array exists
only in layout, so a schematic-level simulation of this block would not be a
simulation of this converter. The transfer, timing and power figures are
measured at five process corners (Table~\ref{tab:adc-corners}) and the nominal
point is \SI{37}{\celsius}, \SI{2.5}{\volt} analog and \SI{1.0}{\volt} digital
supplies. A 100-sample process-and-mismatch Monte Carlo also exists, and its
result is that this netlist cannot produce a matching distribution at all
(Section~\ref{sss:adc-mc}); no gain or offset \(\sigma\) is quoted anywhere
below. The \SI{0.25}{\volt} sweep step is 157 codes. A second sweep of the
unclipped window in \SI{0.0625}{\volt} steps, \num{39} codes apart, carries the
linearity figures, and the corner runs use a 20-point staircase at
\SI{0.1}{\volt}, 63 codes apart. No grid resolves individual codes, so nothing
here is a DNL or INL measurement.
