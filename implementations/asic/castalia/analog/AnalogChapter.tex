\section{Analog Circuitry} \label{s:analog}

\AsicNameForUserGuide{} carries a mixed-signal front end alongside the digital
subsystem documented in the preceding chapters. This chapter describes the analog
blocks themselves and reports their simulated performance; the memory-mapped
registers through which software reaches them are documented with the peripherals
in Section~\ref{s:regMemMap}.

Unless stated otherwise, every figure and table in this chapter is taken from
schematic-level simulation of the block's own testbench --- pre-layout, with no
extracted parasitics --- and is therefore a design-intent characterisation rather
than silicon data. Each block section names the testbench it came from and the
process, temperature and supply points that were simulated, so the coverage behind
each number is explicit. Numbers are regenerated directly from the simulator's
results database rather than transcribed by hand.

\subsection*{Blocks documented in this revision}

The analog front end is being brought up incrementally. The electrochemical models
the benches use are defined once in Section~\ref{ss:analog-models}. This revision documents
the local bias generator, the potentiostat control amplifier it biases, and the
transimpedance stage that reads the working electrode; the reference DAC and the
data converters will be added to this chapter as their benches are closed.

\subsection*{Verification status}

The three blocks are characterised to different depths, and the difference matters when
reading their numbers: a corner run bounds process spread but says nothing about
matching, so any figure whose budget is a mismatch quantity --- offset above all --- is
simply not yet characterised for the two blocks without Monte Carlo.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccl}
\toprule
Block & Section & Benches & Monte Carlo & Principal open item \\
\midrule
Bias generator        & \ref{ss:biasgen-local} & 2 & ---      & no mismatch data \\
Control amplifier     & \ref{ss:controlamp}    & 4 & \checkmark & drive \SI{30}{\percent} short; offset over budget \\
Transimpedance stage  & \ref{ss:tiaamp}        & 5 & ---      & loop peaking; sourcing limit at low gain \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Every block was simulated over the same five process, temperature and supply
points, listed once per section. Load conditions, however, are \emph{not} common: the
control amplifier's small-signal and step figures are taken into \SI{10}{\pico\farad}
and the transimpedance amplifier's into \SI{5}{\pico\farad}, so bandwidth, slew and
settling numbers are not directly comparable between Sections~\ref{ss:controlamp}
and~\ref{ss:tiaamp}. The open items in full:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bias generator} --- corners only. Trim spread and startup margin are
characterised against process but not against mismatch.
\item \textbf{Control amplifier} --- drive averages \SIrange{23.0}{24.4}{\micro\ampere}
against a \SI{33}{\micro\ampere} target, and offset is \(\sigma =
\SI{4.82}{\milli\volt}\) from mismatch alone against a \SI{2}{\milli\volt} budget. Both
are quantified in Section~\ref{ss:controlamp} with the routes to closing them.
\item \textbf{Transimpedance stage} --- four items, all in Section~\ref{ss:tiaamp}: the
closed loop peaks \SIrange{5.0}{6.4}{\decibel} and the feedback capacitor that would
flatten it is impractically large; the class-A output cannot source the full-scale
current at the minimum gain setting; only the maximum gain setting was simulated, with
an ideal feedback resistor rather than the 16-tap string; and no Monte Carlo exists.
Its closed-loop bench also loads the electrode with a bare capacitance rather than the
cell model of Section~\ref{ss:analog-models}, so its peaking is an upper bound.
\end{itemize}
