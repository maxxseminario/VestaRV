\subsubsection{The transimpedance loop}

Closing the loop around the electrode changes the stability picture completely. The
amplifier has \SI{88}{\degree} of phase margin; the transimpedance loop built from it
peaks by \SIrange{5.0}{6.4}{\decibel} at about \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}
(Figure~\ref{fig:tia-zt}). Nothing about the amplifier causes this. Writing
$C_{\mathrm{dl}}$ for the double-layer capacitance at the summing node,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tia-beta}
  \beta(s) = \frac{1}{1 + s R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}}},
  \qquad
  Z_t(s) = \frac{V_{\mathrm{OUT}}}{I_{\mathrm{WE}}}
         = \frac{-R_f}{1 + \dfrac{1 + s R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}}}{A(s)}} .
\end{equation}

At DC the correction term is $1/A_0 = \num{6.1e-5}$, which is why the measured
transimpedance is within \SI{0.02}{\percent} of $R_f$. Above
$1/2\pi R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}} = \SI{0.28}{\hertz}$ the noise gain $1/\beta$ climbs at
\SI{20}{\decibel} per decade and meets the amplifier's own roll-off at

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tia-fx}
  f_x = \sqrt{\frac{f_u}{2 \pi R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}}}} \approx \SI{1.3}{\kilo\hertz},
\end{equation}

a \SI{40}{\decibel}-per-decade rate of closure. The measured peak sits at
\SIrange{1.00}{1.12}{\kilo\hertz}, so the \emph{frequency} is predicted well.

\paragraph{The magnitude is not.} Substituting a single-pole $A(s) = \omega_u/s$ gives a
second-order denominator with

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tia-q}
  \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{\omega_u}{R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}}}},
  \qquad
  Q = \sqrt{\omega_u R_f C_{\mathrm{dl}}} = \num{4700},
\end{equation}

which would be \SI{74}{\decibel} of peaking against \SI{5.06}{\decibel} measured. That
gap is not a modelling detail: the ideal-op-amp TIA result assumes the amplifier drives
the feedback network from zero output impedance, and here
$R_2 = \SI{737}{\kilo\ohm}$ exceeds $R_f = \SI{560}{\kilo\ohm}$, so $R_f$ loads the node
it is fed from and drops the available gain to about \SI{77}{\decibel} before the loop
closes. \textbf{Quantifying this loop needs an \texttt{stb} analysis through the
existing \texttt{IPRB\_F} probe in \texttt{TiaFbTB}, which this run does not contain.}
Until then, the peaking is the measurement and Equations~(\ref{eq:tia-beta})
to~(\ref{eq:tia-q}) are only its mechanism.

\paragraph{Remedy.} A feedback capacitor across $R_f$,
$C_f = \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{dl}} / (2 \pi R_f f_u)}$, flattens the response and sets the
bandwidth to $1/2\pi R_f C_f$. Here that is of order \SI{200}{\pico\farad} ---
\SI{0.1}{\milli\metre\squared} of MIM per channel, and four channels are needed --- and
because $C_f$ scales as $1/\sqrt{R_f}$ the requirement grows at lower gain settings. The
options are an external capacitor on the WE pad, a deliberate reduction of $f_u$ (there
is \SI{88}{\degree} of margin to spend), or accepting the peaking and band-limiting
after the ADC. It should be settled before layout, since two of the three change the
cell.

DC accuracy is not in question: transimpedance within \SI{0.02}{\percent} of $R_f$ at
every corner, incremental gain varying \SI{0.09}{\percent} across the current range, and
a virtual ground holding to \SI{0.4}{\milli\volt}. Below a few hundred hertz the stage
does what it is specified to do.
