\subsection{Reference DAC}\label{ss:dacr2r12}

\texttt{anatop\_pixel\_dacR2R12}, a 12-bit voltage-mode R-2R DAC, produces a channel's
reference potential. It was fed by \texttt{anatop\_pixel\_dac\_psu}, a supply block
deleted from \texttt{anatop\_pixel} on 2026-08-24; the ladder now runs from the
\SI{2.5}{\volt} analog rail directly, and the deleted block is kept on record in
Section~\ref{sss:dacr2r12-supply}. All data in this section is schematic-level, with no
extracted parasitics; layout and extracted views exist for both cells and have not been
simulated.

The ladder is drawn in Figure~\ref{fig:dacr2r12-ladder}. Its terminals are
\texttt{A<11:0>}, \texttt{En} and \texttt{Out}; the supply arrives by inherited
connection and netlists as \texttt{inh\_vdd}/\texttt{inh\_vss}, so there is no supply pin
on the symbol. Each code bit is gated through a two-input
gate whose output high level \emph{is} the ladder reference, so whatever drives
\texttt{inh\_vdd} sets full scale directly. That gate was a 1.0\,V standard cell
until 2026-09-05 and is now a custom 2.5\,V cell; every corner and Monte Carlo
number below is measured on the current one, and the change is recorded once,
after the nominal linearity table. Every element of the ladder is the same unit resistor (\(R\) is two
units in series, \(2R\) four), which makes the binary weighting a ratio of identical
devices and the converter's linearity a matching property rather than a design margin.
A nodal solve of the transcribed network gives \(V_{\mathrm{out}}/V_{\mathrm{ref}} =
D/4096\) exactly at every code, so none of the nonlinearity reported below is the ratio
design.

Three testbench views drive the two cells. \texttt{DacTB} puts the DAC on an ideal
\SI{2.5}{\volt} rail and measures the ladder alone, which is the tapeout arrangement.
\texttt{DacPSUTB} exercises the supply block by itself against a current load, and
\texttt{DacWPSUTB} runs both together with the DAC's inherited supply resolved to
\texttt{VddDac} by a \texttt{netSet} property; both of those measure the removed
block.

Coverage is five corner points (the typical statistical point and the ff, fs, sf and
ss skew corners) and a 200-sample Monte Carlo run varying process and mismatch
together, both on the current cell. Temperature is \SI{25}{\celsius} on both DAC benches throughout; the four
supply-block benches run at \SI{27}{\celsius} at the typical point and \SI{25}{\celsius}
at the skew corners. The two DAC benches carry tightened solver tolerances
(\texttt{reltol}~=~\num{1e-6}, \texttt{vabstol}~=~\num{1e-9},
\texttt{iabstol}~=~\num{1e-15}): the LSB through the supply block is \SI{529}{\micro\volt}
and the ADE defaults permit several LSB of error per DC solve, of which DNL is a
difference.

One of this section's two defects survives into the tapeout channel. The ladder is
matching-limited and fails its own linearity limits on an ideal rail
(Section~\ref{sss:dacr2r12-mc}), and no corner point sees it, or can, because a process
corner moves every ladder resistor together. The other, the supply block modulating the
reference by \SI{25.8}{\milli\volt} peak to peak across the code range against a
quarter-LSB budget of \SI{133}{\micro\volt}, went with the block it belonged to
(Section~\ref{sss:dacr2r12-supply}): it is no longer modelled, not smaller.
