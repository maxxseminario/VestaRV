1 · Three observation tiers — what actually needs a pad

Castalia already observes analog state two ways before any debug fabric exists. Counting them first removes most of the candidate pad list.

TierMechanismReachesCostsLimits
0 — electrode pins CE, RE, WE (and RE2) are bonded per site the three loop nodes, directly, on a package pin nothing — already in the plan a probe puts ~50 pF of cable on a loop node; no isolation, no force arbitration
1 — the SAR per-channel 10-bit SAR through the adcSourceSel input mux any node the mux can be extended to reach, digitised in-system mux inputs + register bits; no pads window 0.441–2.068 V (34.9 % of the 2.5 V range lost to clipping), 1.591 mV effective LSB; blind to anything railed; useless when the SAR itself is the suspect
2 — debug pads analog test bus to dedicated pads nodes with no pin and no SAR path; external precision; force pad-ring width inside the analog island, plus balls every tap is a permanent leakage and capacitance load on the node it observes

Tier 0 means CE, RE and WE do not earn debug-pad slots for observation. They are already pins. What tier 2 adds for them is a buffered, cable-isolated copy — which is a different and defensible product, because probing RE directly hangs a cable on A1's feedback node and A1 has only ~13 dB of loop gain into a 32 kΩ cell. Tier 1 covers routine, in-field, in-system checks at 1.591 mV resolution. Tier 2 must therefore be justified only by nodes that are neither pinned nor SAR-reachable, by measurements needing better than 1.591 mV, by nodes outside the SAR window, and by force.

Rev-1 precedent. Myshkin already shipped this idea at minimum scale: pins 35/36 are ATP-IN and ATP-OUT, controlled by AFE_ATPEN and a one-bit AFE_ATPSEL (potentiostat output vs. DSADC output), with AFE_ADCEXTIN selecting an external pin into the ADC. Rev-2 needs the same two-pad shape with a wider select and a defined force path — not a new class of structure.

2.3 Rejected: breaking Rf out

Bringing the TIA feedback resistor's terminals to pads was considered and rejected. Rf spans exactly the WE summing node and vout, both of which are already observable; the extra pads would buy only the ability to substitute an external resistor, at the cost of putting package inductance and 50 pF of cable inside the transimpedance loop — the loop whose phase margin is already 15–38° into a 100 nF Cdl. The measurement it is wanted for, transimpedance gain per code, is obtained non-invasively: force a known current into the WE pin from an external SMU with no cell attached and read vout. That needs no new pad at all, and it is the recommended Rf calibration primitive. CAL

3 · Pad budget — the real constraint

The frame is not the limit and the package nearly is. Two independent ceilings:

The island is the binding number and it is already oversubscribed: 16 electrode pads plus AVDD and AVSS is 18 slots against 10, so the RING_CUT_W/RING_CUT_E instances must move to 450 µm regardless of anything in this document. Each debug pad is a further 25 µm of island. And every analog pad must stay inside the bracket: PDB3A_G's internal guard strap hard-shorts core VDD to VSS at any abutment with a digital-family pad (WQ-DELTA 14, found by Pegasus FIND_SHORTS at PAD_ARSV0/PAD_AVSS and structurally invisible to DRC and verifyGeometry). Debug pads cannot be tucked into free slots on the other three sides.

Sixteen dedicated debug pins is not affordable — it is impossible. It needs 16 balls and 7 exist. Even ignoring the package, it would take the analog island from 450 µm to 850 µm, pushing every pad on the north side and both ring cuts, on a die whose pad ring is already being re-placed once for the electrodes.

4 · Options and recommendation

C — 2 observe (1 buffered, 1 direct) + 2 force44+100 µm feasible; the force pads duplicate capability already present at the electrode pins
D — recommended — 1 buffered observe + 1 direct sense/force, both shared, with a per-channel 16:1 mux and a chip-level 16:1 mux22+50 µm recommended

4.1 Why two, not four

  • A second force pad has no job. The one force operation that needs current — injecting a known I into WE to calibrate transimpedance per code — is done at the WE pin, with lower series resistance and no leakage path than any on-chip mux could offer. The remaining force targets (vcm, vref, the bias rails, the biasgen override) carry no current and share one wire happily.
  • A Kelvin-return pad has no job. AVSS and AVDD are already pins; muxing vssa_sense/vdda_sense onto the direct pad and reading against board ground yields the IR drop directly.
  • Simultaneous two-node observation is already available. The one pairing that matters is potential and current at the same instant. Put RE on the buffered pad and let the SAR digitise vout: the SAR is the second observation channel, and it is per-channel and free.
  • Both pads have distinct, non-substitutable jobs. The buffered pad is the only loading-safe path to high-impedance nodes. The direct pad is the only metrologically clean path, and the only force path. Neither can be dropped; nothing else survives the same test.
Buffer offset, and the chicken-and-egg. A unity-gain test buffer built from the same 2.5 V devices will carry an offset in the same class as the blocks it observes — A1 measures σ 5.20 mV, A2 σ 8.91 mV. Buffering RE and then calibrating against the buffered value would simply substitute one unknown offset for another. The two-pad split resolves it without a trim: any mux node can exit through either path, so measuring one node both ways gives the buffer's error at that node's level. Sweep vcm across the range and the buffer's offset and gain error are characterised on the same chip, in the same session, before RE is ever read through it. This is the reason the direct pad exists and the reason it must reach the same mux output, not a separate tap.

4.2 Costed upgrade, if the island widens anyway

A third pad — a second buffered observe on an independent mux exit — buys simultaneous observation of two high-impedance nodes (RE and WE during a potential step, for example). It costs 25 µm, one ball of seven, and a second buffer. Take it only if the pad-ring owner is already moving the ring cuts and has the width; it is not required by any procedure in §9. Four pads is the ceiling worth defending; sixteen is not defensible at any island width.

5 · Fabric architecture

5.1 Why the buffer is shared and the muxes are not

One buffer, four channel muxes. The alternative — a buffer per channel — was rejected because the shared on-chip bus is only routing capacitance (order 200 fF, guarded), which a loop already driving a 100 nF electrode does not notice, whereas the pad plus cable is 50 pF and must never reach a high-impedance node. Isolation therefore belongs at the pad, not at the channel. Per-channel muxes are kept because they localise the permanent tap: an off channel's leakage lands on ATB, not on a neighbour's WE.

5.2 Cell reuse — and one correction

anatop_eis_tgate_amux16 (leon AMUXTGDX16A) is not a 16:1 mux. Its terminals are InP InM Clk ClkBar OutP OutM OutN: a differential two-way analog mux at ×16 drive, following the same size convention as TGP16A/TGP2A. The X16 is strength, not fan-in. A 16:1 sense mux must be built from sixteen minimum-strength T-gates plus a decoder, and ×16 devices are the wrong choice anyway — off-state leakage and off-capacitance scale with width, and both are the spec drivers (§7). Build from anatop_tgate (TGP2A), the same primitive already used at ×4/×8 in anatop_tia_rprog_sw. Note that multi, not m, is what reaches the netlist for MOS in this kit.

6 · Register fields

The rev-2 register stubs already reserve the right home: five 16-word blocks — AFE0…AFE3 at 0x4C00/40/80/C0 and EIS at 0x7C00 — with word 0x3 named SWM, "switch-matrix / analog-multiplexer configuration". Naming follows the Myshkin convention: AFE_ bit-field prefix, selects suffixed SEL, enables EN.

Arbitration must not live in the per-site registers. Under the orchestrator each AFE<h> block is owned by a different hart, so four independently-scheduled harts could each drive ATB. Put the grant in the hart-0-only shared block at 0x7C00, which already owns the EIS 5:1 electrode mux, and AND every per-site enable with it in hardware. Contention then cannot be expressed, rather than being a software convention.

The key field is not ceremony — a debug switch that closes during a measurement corrupts a clinical result silently, and this is a medical device; the fabric must be impossible to enable by a stray store.

6.3 Tier-1 field

Extend the rev-1 one-bit AFE_ATPSEL/AFE_ADCEXTIN pair into a single per-site AFE_ADCSRCSEL[2:0] in ADCC (word 0x4): 0 = vout (normal), 1 = vcm (zero-code calibration, the existing pattern), 2 = vref, 3 = RE (the RE-tap fallback the EIS plan already anticipates), 4 = ATB, 5–7 reserved. Code 4 is the one that pays for itself twice: any debug-mux node becomes digitisable on-chip with no external instrument, and an external ramp on atb_d becomes a per-chip measurement of the SAR transfer — the 1.535× slope, the 0.441 V code-0 intercept and the 1.591 mV effective LSB are currently schematic-simulation numbers with no in-system check.

7 · Off-state, leakage and disturb specification

A disabled mux input is a permanent parasitic on a loop node. These are the numbers it must meet, each derived from a measured quantity rather than a rule of thumb.

Off-state leakage, tap on RE≤ 20 pA at 40 °C through Rs+Rct = 1 MΩ this is 20 µV, 1.3 % of the 1.591 mV ADC LSB

Bus AC amplitude while a neighbour acquiresprocedural 1 fF at 10 kHz and 0.1 V is 6 pA — approaching the WE budget. Rule: do not drive a large AC signal on ATB while another channel acquires at max gain

The EIS plan's open item 5 states the identical spec driver — "leakage at RE" — for its 4-terminal 5:1 electrode mux. The two fabrics should share a leakage specification and a switch cell, but must not be merged: the EIS mux carries up to 15 µA of excitation, the debug mux carries fA to nA of sense current, and one set of device sizes cannot be right for both.

8 · ESD and pad-ring rules

  • Cell. PDB3A_G, the same analog pad as the electrodes, on the same TAVDD/TAVSS analog ESD bus. PDB3AC_G exists and would give the debug pads an independent ESD bus (TACVDD/TACVSS), isolating a debug-pad strike from the electrode rail — but it costs two more supply pads inside a 25 µm-per-slot island. Not recommended; record it as the fallback if a strike path through the electrode rail proves to be a problem.
  • Series resistance. ≥ 200 Ω of rppolywo between each pad and the first T-gate. The switches are 2.5 V thin-oxide devices and must never be the ESD conduction path; the resistor gives CDM current somewhere else to go. It is also harmless to the measurement — the direct pad drives a high-impedance meter, and the buffer is inside the pad's resistor, not outside it.
  • Reachability. Because ATB can reach RE and WE through an on switch, an ESD event at either pad has a conduction path into a loop node. This is why the reset state is all-open and why the enables are power-good gated: an unpowered or resetting chip presents no path at all.
  • Latch-up. The force pad sits metres of cable from an SMU next to the most sensitive amplifiers on the die. Specify ±100 mA per JESD78, and state in the bring-up procedure that every force operation runs with the source's compliance set to 10 mA or less.
  • Placement. Inside the PRCUT_G bracket, in the AVSS · PDB3A · AVDD motif proven on Myshkin and the quad cut. Never abutting a digital-family pad (WQ-DELTA 14).
  • Routing. ATB and both pad nets guarded, and not routed under or over the electrode pads.

9 · Bring-up and dead-channel triage

The fabric's value is that four failure modes which look identical at the SAR — a stuck code — become distinguishable in six settings. Run in order; each step's result invalidates everything below it if it fails.

9.2 Calibration sequence enabled

  1. Characterise the buffer: mux vcm, read it on atb_d and on atb_o, sweep the vcm code across the range. Difference = buffer offset and gain error. CAL
  2. Characterise the SAR: ADCSRCSEL=ATB, ramp atb_d externally, fit slope and intercept per chip. CAL
  3. Characterise vref: direct read at a set of codes, no loop involved. CAL
  4. Measure the applied-potential error: RE through the buffer against the vref code, per site. This is A1's offset on this die, the term with 0 % four-channel yield against ±1 mV. CAL
  5. Measure transimpedance per code: force I into the WE pin with no cell, read vout on atb_d (not through the SAR — the SAR window truncates the railing samples). CAL
  6. Zero-current offset: mux vcm into the SAR, existing pattern, with atb_d as the fallback for chips whose vout lands outside 0.441–2.068 V. CAL

Steps 1–2 must precede 4–6: the instrument is calibrated before the circuit. A parallel effort is writing the per-chip calibration procedure into the TRM; the signals it needs are the six marked CAL in §2, and the fabric above is a superset of them.

10.2 Contradictions with the existing documents

  1. 12 vs 16 electrode pads. CLAUDE.md and AfeSystemDiagram.tex both say twelve (CE/RE/WE per site). The package model in platform/common/python/generate.py and the QFN-64 padlist both bond sixteen, adding an RE2 Kelvin sense per site at pins 78–93. This document assumes sixteen, because that is what the pinout allocates — and it matters: at twelve there are eleven spare balls, at sixteen there are seven. Resolve before the ring cuts move.
  2. The Rf string. afe_rev2_strategy.html still describes a 16-tap 35 kΩ string giving ±33 µA … ±2 µA, and separately proposes extending it to 38 taps. Both are superseded by anatop_tia_rprog: 18 kΩ + 6 kΩ·N, N = 0…319, measured 19.65 kΩ–1.9308 MΩ, ±40.7 µA down to ±0.414 µA at 0.824 nA/LSB. The numbers in this document use the built cell.
  3. The SAR window. The strategy document quotes ±1.15 V of usable swing and a 2.441 mV LSB. The measured transfer is 0.441–2.068 V — ±0.815 V about a 1.255 V centre — with a 1.591 mV effective LSB and 34.9 % of the input range lost to clipping. Every range argument here, including the case for a vout pad, uses the measured window.
  4. anatop_eis_tgate_amux16 is not a 16:1 mux (§5.2). Any plan that assumed it as a drop-in fan-in cell needs revising; the ×16 is drive strength.
  5. Bias-net collision. The chip-level mux slots name the AFE globals bn!/bnc!/bp!/bpc!. The EIS island drives bnm!/bnc!/bpm!/bpc! and bnc!/bpc! collide (EIS open item 1). If that is resolved by renaming, the slot map in §2.2 changes with it.
  6. adcSourceSel is a schematic net name, not a register field. Its rev-1 register counterparts are AFE_ADCEXTIN and AFE_ADCSEL. §6.3 names the rev-2 field AFE_ADCSRCSEL; the digital owner should confirm before the prose in the strategy and EIS documents propagates the net name into the register map.

11 · Slot map (2026-08-22)

Every mux input is named or explicitly spare. The map merges §2's inventory with the calibration procedure's interface list (the TRM per-block measurement paragraphs, the per-block note_meas_*.tex fragments) and the EIS-lite plan's requirement that RE be buffered at source. Selects are the approved fields: per-channel AFE_DBGSEL[3:0], chip-level AFE_DBGGSEL[6:3], arbitration AFE_DBGOWNSEL[2:0].

11.1 The mux cell — imported, not built

§5.2 sized a build-from-anatop_tgate mux because no N:1 analog mux was known to exist. One does. leon/MUX16A is a 16:1 transmission-gate tree with a straight binary select. It is instantiated in leon's own AFE hierarchy (AFE_TB/config_ams binds it) and inherited from smurray5's teewinot, 2020 — the direct ancestor of AFE_ECS — with av_extracted views in both workspaces. Its live instance headers resolve entirely inside leon and tsmcN65 — no teewinot dependency survives. It was copied into castalia on 2026-08-22 by skill/castalia_amux_copy.il, schematic + symbol + layout, with the leon-internal headers repointed onto the copies in both the schematic and the layout hierarchy.

90 MOS per 16:1, 450 across the five muxes, no decoder. The select is straight binary — traced instance by instance: anatop_amux_2 passes A0 at Sel = 0; anatop_amux_4 drives its two input muxes from Sel<0> and the output mux from Sel<1>; anatop_amux_16 gives its four first-stage muxes A<0:3>, <4:7>, <8:11>, <12:15> in order and selects between them on Sel<3:2>. So AFE_DBGSEL = n selects A<n>, with no encoding table to get wrong. Verified after the copy in a fresh session: 3 cells, 9 views, 0 unbound instances and 0 foreign-library references in schematic and layout.

Layout caveat. The geometry copied byte-for-byte — identical bounding boxes and identical shape counts per layer number at all three levels — but castalia is bound to cdsDefTechLib, not tsmcN65, so those layer numbers resolve to no name in this library (leon binds its own leon tech). The numbers are the standard TSMC set (3 NW, 6 OD, 17 PO, 18 OD_25, 25 PP, 26 NP, 30 CO, 31–33 M1–M3), so nothing is lost — but the imported layouts are not DRC- or LVS-usable until the technology library is re-attached. Schematic netlisting and simulation are unaffected, which is the same position every other anatop_* layout in this library is already in.

Why the tree beats the flat 16-tap star of §5.2. In a star, all sixteen off switches share the bus node, so ATB carries 16 off-capacitances and 16 leakage paths. In the tree the bus sees only the final anatop_amux_2 — two T-gates — and each input still sees exactly one. The tree also needs no 4:16 decoder: the ~170 devices a decoded star would spend on AND gates and inverters are the same devices the tree spends on switching. The cost is Ron: the selected path crosses four T-gates in series, ≈ 5 kΩ, which is nothing against a voltmeter but must be added to the ≥ 200 Ω pad resistor and the ≥ 1 kΩ vout series resistor when sizing settling.

The one place the imported cell does not meet §7 as drawn: its pass devices are 4 µm wide, where §7 asks for minimum strength. Estimated off-capacitance is 5–8 fF per T-gate against a 20 fF budget, so it passes with margin on the estimate — but it is an estimate. Off-leakage at 40 °C remains unmeasured (§10.1 item 4), and the retirement sim is now specific: DC leakage into A<7> of anatop_amux_16 at 40 °C with the other fifteen inputs at rails, against 20 pA. Narrow the leaf devices only if that fails; halving them halves both parasitics and doubles Ron, which nothing in this document would notice.

11.4 What the map changes

  • RE is buffered at source, not switched. §2.1 gave RE a tap and §7 gave that tap a ≤ 20 pA budget. The EIS-lite plan already requires four per-site unity-gain RE buffers — anatop_eis_ota_p is the characterised candidate, AOL 76.6 dB, offset −87.6 µV, 28.3 µA — because a series T-gate on a reference electrode leaves its off-leakage there permanently. Slot 6 takes the buffer output. The RE leakage line of §7 is retired for this fabric and becomes a buffer-input-current spec (< 100 µV of RE shift) owned by the EIS plan's V-3x sim. One buffer, two documents, no duplication.
  • Slot 0 is a reset park, not a signal. A T-gate tree always connects one input to Y; only AFE_DBGEN and the grant keep Y off ATB. Putting local ground at code 0000 makes the all-zeroes state harmless twice over instead of once.
  • The working electrode gets two slots, not one. the TRM per-block measurement paragraphs C1 adds series isolation switches in all three electrode paths, which splits the old WE node into a summing node and a pad node. Slots 7 and 8 straddle that switch, and their difference under a forced current is the Ron the calibration procedure has to store.
  • The selects are 2.5 V. anatop_amux_2 carries its own inverter on vdd!/vss!, so Sel is a 2.5 V logic input. Twenty select bits (4 per site × 4, plus 4 chip-level) cross the 1.0 V → 2.5 V boundary and need anatop_lvl_d10a25 shifters, as do AFE_DBGEN, the grant and the force enables. That is a real cell count inside the analog island and it belongs in the floorplan estimate; it is not new — every existing AFE control bit already crosses the same boundary.
  • No slot is spent on Rcal. the TRM per-block measurement paragraphs C4's on-chip gain reference resistor sits between the counter-electrode node and the working-electrode summing node, and both ends are already slots 5 and 7. §2.3's rejection of Rf pads applies unchanged.

11.5 Carried forward

  • §10.1 items 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 are untouched by this map. Item 4 narrows to the two questions above: the series guard at slot 7, and a leakage number for anatop_amux_16 at 40 °C.
  • The bias-net collision still stands: chip-level slots 2–5 name bn!/bnc!/bp!/bpc!, and if that collision with the EIS island's bnm!/bnc!/bpm!/bpc! is resolved by renaming, those four rows change with it.
  • Correction to §5.2. Its conclusion is right — the ×16 in anatop_eis_tgate_amux16 is drive strength, not fan-in — but the terminal list quoted there (InP InM Clk ClkBar OutP OutM OutN) is stale text left in the OA file by the cell's ChopperTGDX16A ancestry. The live interface is A B X S SN: a single-ended 2:1 analog mux with a complementary select, two anatop_eis_tgate_d16 instances. Neither reading makes it a 16:1.
  • At 12 electrode pads and 2 ATB pads, 9 of the 11 spare balls remain. The §4.2 third pad is still affordable and still not required.
