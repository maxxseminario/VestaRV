Castalia AFE Rev-2 — Bipolar Potentiostat Strategy

Revision plan for the Myshkin wound-monitoring front end, targeting the quad-core Castalia chip. Surveyed from the Myshkin tapeout LVS netlists, 2026-07-20.

Rev-2 goals

  • 4 identical AFE channels (Myshkin had 1), one per Castalia core.
  • SAR ADC only — eliminate the dual-slope ADC (dualSlope_sch4lay) and its trim buses.
  • Bipolar current sensing — each 3-terminal potentiostat must measure both anodic and cathodic (positive and negative) cell current. This is the critical requirement.
  • Favor simple over complex topologies.

Root cause of the one-sided limit: the PMOS-only mirror can only source current into CE and only copy sourced current. When the electrochemical cell demands current in the opposite direction, the output stage cuts off and the control loop rails. The amplifiers themselves are not the limit — the folded-cascode output already sources and sinks.

Chosen topology: grounded-WE two-amp potentiostat

Delete the mirror and sense at the working electrode instead of the counter electrode. Same architecture as the LMP91000 / AD5940 — the textbook simple bipolar potentiostat, and subtractive relative to rev-1.

Why this wins

  • Bipolar for free: anodic current puts vout below vcm, cathodic above. The single-ended 10-bit SAR needs zero changes — data is offset-binary around the vcm code; subtract in firmware, or occasionally mux vcm into the ADC (existing adcSourceSel pattern) to calibrate the zero code.
  • Simpler and more accurate: removing the mirror removes its mismatch and channel-length-modulation gain error. The sensed current is exact by KCL.
  • Maximum reuse: both amps, the R string, both DACs, the bias generator, level shifters, and the SAR carry over unchanged.

Rf sizing against the four-marker panel

The 10-bit SAR is fixed. Over the ±1.155 V usable swing against a 2.441 mV code that is 473 usable codes per polarity — a 473:1 (2.68-decade) window whose width cannot be changed by Rf. Rf only slides the window along the current axis, so

sensitivity = IFS / 473

and the only lever is to make IFS no larger than that channel must digitize. Each channel owns one marker at one formal potential, so each sees only its own charging baseline plus its own peak — the ceilings are per channel, and all four sit above the 560 kΩ top tap:

Because +0x2 TIA in the frozen 16-word per-site map already carries the gain-range select per site, per-channel taps cost no digital-contract change — they are firmware.

The string change

Keep the 35 kΩ unit segment and lengthen the string: 38 × 35 kΩ = 35 kΩ … 1.33 MΩ, tap select 4 bits → 6 bits. This preserves every existing tap including the 35 kΩ / ±33 µA end, keeps the unit device identical for matching and trim, and lands within 1–3 % of the ideal tap on all four channels. Alternatives hit nearly the same numbers but discard the low-gain end and change the unit segment, so they are strictly worse for the same area:

Cost is ~2.4× the poly in the Rf bank, which the CA0 spec already flags as the dominant contributor to the 0.03–0.05 mm² TIA. Noise is a non-issue at every tap: the amp's output-referred 191 µVrms (implied by 341.5 pArms at 560 kΩ) means input-referred current noise falls as 1/Rf, holding 0.08 LSB throughout — raising Rf improves noise and costs only full scale. Two things need re-verification, since the TRM characterizes only the 560 kΩ tap: the Cf pole moves (22 pF at 1.33 MΩ puts it at 5.5 kHz against 12.9 kHz at 560 kΩ — still far above the 10–500 Hz DPV pulse rate, but re-run the bench), and phase margin rises with Rf, so stability is not the risk here.

What Rf cannot fix. Best achievable LSB at 10 bits is IFS/473 = 2.1–3.9 nA per channel. The modeled LOD steps — 20 % above the reagent blank — are 1.0 nA (CEA), 1.4 (CA19-9), 1.8 (HE4), 2.4 (CA-125), landing at 0.47–0.90 LSB; and DPV peak height is a difference of two conversions, so quantization on ΔI is √2 worse again at 3.0–5.4 nA. LOD-level detection is closed to Rf at 10 bits on every channel, at any value. The routes to it are more bits, or a baseline-cancellation current DAC at the TIA summing node — subtracting the 236–509 nA charging residual before conversion would allow Rf ≈ 10 MΩ and buy roughly a decade. What the string change does deliver is resolving a 10 % concentration change at each clinical cut-off, from 2.5–7 LSB today to 8–12 LSB, over the full plotted concentration range with no mid-sweep gain change.

Provenance: baselines and peak currents are the analytic DPV and four-parameter-logistic models in ~/work/ieee/ISCAS27/latek/graphics-data/gen_figdata.py, parameterized from published assay literature — no point is measured, from silicon or from a wet assay. The 1.0 nA CEA requirement follows entirely from the modeled i_bg = 5 nA blank and moves with the real blank current. Sizing added 2026-08-18.

To verify in simulation (both expected fine at µA-scale wound currents)

  • bts_pmos output stage is class-A on the source side (current-source PMOS pull-up MM5) — check worst-case cathodic full-scale drive; if short, bump MM5's bias rather than going class-AB.
  • folded_cascode_pmos previously drove only a MOS gate; it now supplies the actual cell current through CE. Confirm its class-A source/sink limits against max expected wound current; add a buffer only if needed.
  • Loop stability of both amps with electrode impedance models (RE is now inside A1's feedback loop; WE capacitance loads A2's virtual ground). Done for A2 — it does not pass. See the next section.

TIA feedback compensation — the Cf decision

TB-03 (anatop_tb_tia, run 2026-07-28) measured the A2 transimpedance loop with Rf = 560 kΩ and 1 µF at WE. The closed-loop transimpedance is not flat: it peaks 5.0–6.4 dB at about 1 kHz across the five corners. The stage needs a feedback capacitor, an external one, or a deliberate bandwidth restriction. Which of the three is an open decision, and it changes the cell — so it has to be settled before layout.

This is not an amplifier defect. A2 has 88° of phase margin in unity feedback with a 5 pF load. That is a different loop. The transimpedance loop's feedback network is Rf working against the electrode capacitance, and its stability has nothing to do with the unity-gain number. Confusing the two is the standard way this is missed.

1 · Which nodes Cf goes between

Cf sits in parallel with the whole feedback path: from A2's inverting input — the WE summing node, pin vinm on anatop_tiaamp — to A2's output, pin vout. The same two nodes Rf already spans.

2 · How the error varies with frequency

The error is zero at DC, negligible to about 100 Hz, then grows fast to a resonant peak at 1 kHz before the response collapses. Measured, tt corner, Rf = 560 kΩ:

Frequency|Zt|Error vs Rf
1 Hz559.9 kΩ—DC accuracy set by Rf alone
126 Hz565.5 kΩ+1 %end of the usable flat band
282 Hz588.6 kΩ+5 %
1.00 kHz1002 kΩ+79 % (5.06 dB)peak
1.71 kHz396 kΩ−29 %the quoted −3 dB point, past the peak
2.82 kHz110 kΩ−80 %

The mechanism is a feedback-path problem, not a signal-path one. Cdl sits at the summing node, where the signal is an injected current, so it does not filter the signal — it attenuates the feedback:

β(s) = 1 / (1 + s·Rf·Cdl)

Above 1/(2π·Rf·Cdl) = 0.28 Hz the fed-back fraction falls at 20 dB/decade, so the noise gain 1/β climbs at 20 dB/decade. A2's own open-loop gain is falling at 20 dB/decade past its 290 Hz dominant pole. The two meet at a 40 dB/decade rate of closure — each slope contributing 90° of lag, so ≈180° total and almost no phase margin. Peaking is what that looks like in the closed-loop magnitude.

Consequences, in the order they will bite:

  • Gain error against frequency — a 1 kHz current reads 1.8× high. Any EIS sweep through that band is wrong.
  • Ringing on potential steps — chronoamperometry and CV apply steps; the current transient rings at ~1 kHz, lengthening settling.
  • Excess noise — the noise-gain peak amplifies A2's input-referred noise around 1 kHz, above what the corner noise table implies.
  • It rings, it does not oscillate. 5–6 dB of peaking is underdamped, not unstable.

3 · Sizing — and why the number is not settled

The textbook value is Cf = √( Cdl / (2π·Rf·fu) ), which also sets the resulting flat bandwidth to 1/(2π·Rf·Cf). At fu = 6.387 MHz and Cdl = 1 µF, across the R string:

Rf tapCfFlat bandwidthMIM area / channel× 4 channels% of die
35 kΩ (min gain)844 pF5.4 kHz0.42 mm²1.69 mm²19.3 %
140 kΩ422 pF2.7 kHz0.21 mm²0.84 mm²9.6 %
560 kΩ (max gain)211 pF1.3 kHz0.11 mm²0.42 mm²4.8 %

MIM at 2 fF/µm²; die 2960 × 2960 µm = 8.76 mm².

Two scaling laws drive the decision, and both are counter-intuitive:

  • Cf ∝ 1/√Rf — the lowest gain setting needs the largest capacitor. A single fixed Cf sized for the 35 kΩ tap costs 19 % of the die across four channels.
  • Cf ∝ √Cdl — 10 nF of electrode wants 21 pF, 10 µF wants 667 pF. A 30× spread over a plausible electrode range.
Do not commit to these values. The same single-pole model that produces them predicts Q = 4741, i.e. 73.5 dB of peaking at Rf = 560 kΩ, against 5.06 dB measured — an effective Q of 1.79. The model is wrong by 68 dB on the quantity Cf is meant to fix, most likely because A2's second-stage output resistance (737 kΩ) is comparable to Rf and it does not drive the feedback network from a low impedance. Treat the table as an order of magnitude. TiaFbTB already contains an IPRB_F probe and no stb analysis was ever run through it; that measurement — minutes of simulation — is the prerequisite for sizing anything.

4 · The options

OptionCostVerdict
External Cf on the board, WE pad to a vout pad Needs vout brought out per channel — +4 pads on top of the 12 electrode pads. Zero die area, no MIM. Strongest technically — the user matches Cf to their actual electrode and gain setting, which is the only way to track a Cdl that is not a chip parameter. Gated on the pad budget.
Digitally programmable on-chip bank, slaved to the 16-tap gain code Binary bank spanning ≈4× to track Cf ∝ 1/√Rf. At the model's numbers, 0.42–1.69 mm² for four channels. No new pads. Viable only if the stb shows the real Cf is far below the model's. Tracks Rf automatically but cannot track Cdl — sized for one electrode class.
Fixed on-chip Cf Sized for the worst tap: 844 pF × 4 = 19 % of the die. Rejected on area, and wrong at 15 of the 16 gain settings.
Accept the peaking, correct after the ADC Free. Rejected: the correction depends on Cdl, which is unknown at design time and drifts as the electrode fouls. Does not fix the ringing or the excess noise either.
Reduce fu via the existing 6-bit TCBias tail trim No new silicon — the knob already exists. Lowers the peak, but fx ∝ √fu falls with it, so flat bandwidth is traded away. Not a standalone fix (peaking Q ∝ √fu falls far too slowly), but worth sweeping alongside a Cf — it is free and A2 is heavily overcompensated for this application.
Next step, in order: (1) run the stb analysis through the existing IPRB_F probe in TiaFbTB to get a real phase margin and loop gain for this loop; (2) sweep Rf × Cdl two-dimensionally to map peaking against gain setting and electrode, since both dominate over any process variation; (3) size Cf from the measurement, then pick external vs. programmable on the resulting area and the pad budget. Monte Carlo on transistor mismatch is the least informative axis for this particular spec and should follow, not precede, the stb.

Alternatives considered and rejected

TopologyWhy rejected
Complementary NMOS + PMOS mirror (push-pull sense) Doubles the matching problem; crossover distortion exactly at zero current — the most interesting region for redox monitoring.
Polarity-switched / commutated front end (T-gate swap) Needs polarity detection, glitches through zero crossings, useless for CV-style sweeps. Only viable for known-polarity DC.

Four-channel plan for Castalia

  • Per channel (×4, identical): control amp + TIA + R string + own 10-bit SAR. Per-channel SARs keep the channels truly identical and allow simultaneous sampling — cleaner than a 4:1 mux into a shared SAR.
  • R string length: extend from 16 to 38 × 35 kΩ (35 kΩ–1.33 MΩ) and select a different tap per channel — 630 k / 805 k / 910 k / 1120 k for CA-125 / HE4 / CA19-9 / CEA. See the sizing section above: the four channels sit at different formal potentials and therefore have different full-scale requirements, and a common 560 kΩ tap wastes up to 2.1× of the fixed 10-bit window on three of them. Tap select widens 4 bits → 6 bits; the per-site +0x2 TIA register already holds it.
  • Shared: BiasGenCascWideSwingGlobal (designed as a global distributor) and the vcm DAC.
  • Open decision: one shared vref DAC (all channels at the same cell potential) vs. per-channel vref (4× Dac2V5R2R14, or one DAC + sample/holds).
  • Pads: each channel now needs a WE pin in addition to CE/RE (rev-1 assumed WE grounded off-chip) → 12 electrode pads total. Check against the Castalia pad budget early (quad die is 2960 × 2960 µm).
  • Deleted with the dual slope: dualSlope_sch4lay, its comparator, one MUX2A leg, and the lbDS/tbDS/rc_en/rcap_en trim buses.
