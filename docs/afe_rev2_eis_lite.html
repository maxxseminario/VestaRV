1 · Verdict

The existing potentiostat chain does EIS over the whole diagnostic band, at ±1 % magnitude and ±0.6° phase to 5.6 kHz and ±5 % / ±2.9° to 28 kHz, across a |Z| span of 246 Ω – 12.1 MΩ at 10 mV excitation. The fouling signature it is meant to track (Rct∥Cdl arc, fc ≈ 0.02–160 Hz per afe_rev2_eis.html §10) sits two to four decades inside that. One piece of new analog is required — a buffered RE tap into adcSourceSel — and it is required because of the reference DAC, not because of the amplifiers.

Frequency band0.1 Hz – 5.6 kHz at ±1 % / ±0.6°; 5.6 – 28 kHz at ±5 % / ±2.9°. Degradation is a smooth 20 dB/decade, not a cliff.
|Z| span246 Ω (N = 0, full compliance) … 12.1 MΩ (N = 319, 1 LSB of AC output) at 10 mV. 100 Ω needs the excitation dropped to 4 mV.
New analog4 × unity-gain RE buffer + one adcSourceSel input + one calibration impedance. No new amplifier class, no new ADC, no new DAC.
New digitalA clock divider / two-code toggle for the excitation, and the I/Q accumulate. Both already budgeted in the 0x7C00 EIS register block.
Sweep cost≈ 2 min for 0.1 Hz – 10 kHz at 5 pts/decade, 90 % of it in the bottom decade; ≈ 60–70 mJ at the measured 197–250 µA/channel.
RecommendationOption (d) — ship potentiostat-EIS; the anatop_eis_* island stays library IP and does not enter the floor plan. §9.
Everything below is derived, not measured. No AC tone has ever been superimposed on v_pattern in any bench in the corpus (grep -riE 'EIS|impedance spectro|lock-?in' over the TRM analog fragments returns zero hits). The loop model of §5 is validated against ten measured operating points to +0.4 / −4 dB and against the standalone TIA bench to 1.4 % in bandwidth and 0.04 dB in peaking — but it is a model. §9 names the three sims that turn it into a measurement.

3 · Excitation — what the DAC can put on RE

The measured DAC is not a clean AC source, and cannot be made into one.

Delivered LSB528.646 µV typ (486.7–572.8 µV corners; MC 528.96 µV, σ 13.88). A 10 mV tone amplitude is 18.9 codes; 4 mV is 7.6 codes.
Static linearityMC INL worst 1.490 LSB mean / 3.453 worst; DNL min −1.737 mean, −6.608 worst. A single 6.6 LSB code step is 3.5 mV against a 10 mV tone.
Reference modulationThe R-2R reference current follows the bit pattern, not the code: Iref spans 0 … 1.444 Vref/R with the maximum at code 1365. Peak-to-peak VddDac excursion over a code sweep 25.835 mV (MC, σ 2.302) against a 133 µV budget — 0 % yield. 99.5 % of the delivered block's static error is this one mechanism.
Settling / glitchNever measured. note_dacr2r12_scope.tex: the code bus is a static busset12 model, so every DAC number in the TRM is a DC solve. Update rate, glitch impulse and the major-carry glitch are unknown.
Output impedance894.3 Ω (MC, σ 46.6), flat within 0.4 Ω from DC to 100 kHz. Harmless — RE and A1's input draw no current, so the static load never changes.

The reference modulation is the disqualifying term. It is a function of the code word, so it is coherent with the excitation — it lands in the demodulated phasor as a systematic, exactly where averaging cannot reach it. A 19-code excursion straddling a major carry flips up to twelve bits, and the reference moves millivolts in sympathy with the tone.

Square wave, two codes, and measure RE. A two-code square uses exactly two bit patterns, so DNL, INL and the reference droop each take exactly two values and the excitation is a perfect square between two unknown but constant levels. Tapping RE into the SAR then makes those levels observed rather than assumed, and the impedance becomes a ratio of two readings through the same converter — the DAC drops out of the accuracy budget entirely, and so does A1's residual loop error (§4). A sine synthesised through this DAC is strictly worse: it visits ~19 code words, each with its own droop, and buys nothing that the firmware demodulator does not already provide.

Fundamental amplitude is 4A/π = 1.273 A, so a ±10 mV square delivers a 12.73 mV fundamental while the interface excursion stays at 10 mV — 1.27× better tone per unit of nonlinearity than a sine, against the 25.9 mV kT/q pseudo-linearity limit. Odd harmonics at 3f, 5f, 7f (1/3, 1/5, 1/7) are a bonus, not a contaminant: demodulating one record at f, 3f, 5f and 7f yields four points from one acquisition, which is worth roughly 3× of sweep time in the sub-Hz decade where records cost tens of seconds. Firmware must correlate against a sine reference; see §7 for why.

5 · Measured current — A2's loop, and the design equation

The TIA is where the frequency limit lives, and it has one clean form. The transimpedance error is 1/T with T = A(s)·β, and the noise gain 1/β is set by Rf against the electrode impedance seen at the summing node:

1/β(f) = 1 + Z_f(f)/Z_el(f)      Z_el = R_u + R_ct || 1/(jωC_dl)      Z_f = R_f/(1+jωR_f C_f)

ε(f) ≈ 1/|T(f)| = (1 + R_f/|Z|) · f / GBW        GBW = 22.78 MHz (A2 measured, tab_ampab_openloop)

The consequence is the useful one. Gain-ranging already holds Rf/|Z| roughly constant: Rf is chosen so the AC current fills the converter, which fixes the ratio at about 40 (half the ±0.80 V compliance, leaving the other half for the DC baseline). The accuracy band therefore does not depend on Cdl, on Rct, or on which gain code is selected — it depends only on A2's gain–bandwidth:

Why the DUT does not set the band. The obvious fear — that the instrument's transfer function depends on Cdl, which is the thing being measured — is real in principle and near-absent in practice. Cdl enters through Zel, but so does Ru: above 1/(2πRuCdl) the electrode looks like its series solution resistance and the noise gain stops climbing at 1 + Rf/Ru. This is why the 12-cell electrode grid measures PM 89.3–93.3° with no Cf at all across Cdl 0.1–10 µF. Gain-ranging then pins the ratio. The residual DUT dependence is through Ru, which EIS measures directly and which is far more stable than Cdl.

7 · Detection — coherent demodulation on the SAR

Sampling is not a constraint. The SAR converts at 869.6 kS/s from a 20 MHz trigger, 23 clocks per conversion, 44.31–55.27 pJ per conversion. At 10 kHz that is 87 samples per cycle; at 5.6 kHz, 155.

DemodulatorFirmware or NPU: I = Σ v[k]·sin(2πfkTs), Q = Σ v[k]·cos(…), over an integer number of excitation periods. 2N MACs per point; at N = 4096 this is beneath notice for either the core or the NPU.
Reference waveformSine, not square — even though the excitation is a square. A sine reference measures Z at exactly one frequency and rejects the excitation's own odd harmonics. §8 quantifies what square-in/square-out detection costs instead.
Process gain√N against white noise. Measured system input-referred current noise (0.1 Hz–1 kHz, electrode grid) is 3.9–21.4 nA rms; a 4 s record at f ≥ 1 Hz brings the detection-band figure to ≲ 1 nA, giving 45–144 dB of SNR on the tone in every case in §5's sweep. Noise never binds inside the band.
QuantisationNot the floor. At high gain the front end delivers 1.7–11.4 mV rms of output noise — 1 to 7 LSB — which self-dithers the converter. At low gain the AC signal itself spans hundreds of codes. No dither circuit is needed; confirm at the low-gain / low-Cdl corner (0.186 mV = 0.12 LSB) where dither is thinnest.
AliasingA square excitation's harmonics fold only above 435 kHz, i.e. from the 87th harmonic upward at a 5 kHz fundamental — ≤ −39 dB intrinsically, further attenuated by A2. Below −60 dB. The TIA's own roll-off is the anti-alias filter.
Record time4 cycles minimum, decimating to keep N manageable: 40 s at 0.1 Hz, 4 s at 1 Hz, then N-limited at ~5 ms above 100 Hz. A 0.1 Hz–10 kHz sweep at 5 pts/decade is ≈ 110 s, of which 98 s is the bottom decade. Demodulating the square's 3rd, 5th and 7th harmonics from the same record cuts that decade ~3×.
Low-frequency floorSet by 1/f noise and drift within the record, not by the front end — the lock-in only rejects noise further than 1/Tmeas from the carrier. Nothing in the corpus measures A2 below 0.1 Hz. Treat 0.1 Hz as the specified floor and measure the real one.

The comparison is not close on anything except the top decade of frequency. The island's genuine advantage is architectural — high-impedance differential sense sidesteps the Rf·Cdl noise gain entirely — and it is exactly the advantage that has not yet been demonstrated in silicon or in simulation, because its sense amplifier measures 7.9× short of the bandwidth that advantage requires.

9 · Recommendation, risks, retirement sims

Recommendation: option (d)

Build potentiostat-EIS now. Keep the anatop_eis_* island as library IP, out of the floor plan, as a bolt-on if silicon shows the need. Options (a) and (d) differ only in whether the copied cells are deleted; they should not be — the copy is done, the benches are copied, and it costs nothing to leave 26 schematic cells in a library. What (d) refuses is floor-plan area, a 4-terminal MUX, a cal network, a clock-phase unit, and the bias-net rework, for a frequency decade whose diagnostic value is unestablished (§11 Q1) and whose delivery is unproven.

Option (b) is rejected on verification state: the island cannot be netlisted as an engine today. Option (c) is rejected on cost-per-decade — carrying two instruments to gain 5.6 kHz → 100 kHz, when the entire stated fouling signature is below 160 Hz.

What "build it" means concretely. Four unity-gain RE buffers with the mux downstream; one calibration impedance per site (a known R plus a known R∥C, T-gate selected between CE and WE — it calibrates Zt(f), the Cf pole and the whole sampled phase chain in one step, and it is worth building even under option (b)); an excitation divider and two-code toggle in the 0x7C00 block; and the I/Q accumulate in firmware. The 48 config bits of §5 of afe_rev2_eis.html shrink to roughly a dozen.

This is the analysis to run first — the chain is linear and time-invariant about its DC bias, so AC gives the exact answer at a fraction of the cost of TRAN, and it is directly comparable to the analytic Z.
STB Through IPRB1 (control loop) and IPRB_F (TIA loop), same variable sweep. Confirms the 1/|T| identity that §5's design equation rests on, and the phase margin at every gain code with the real Rf.
TRAN confirmatory Two-code square on v_pattern at 1 Hz / 100 Hz / 1 kHz / 10 kHz, ≥ 8 cycles, real DAC in the loop. Demodulate in the calculator: i_re = integ(vout*sin(2*pi*f*time)), i_im = integ(vout*cos(...)) over an integer number of periods, compared against the analytic Z. The only thing AC cannot show: the real DAC waveform, the sampled/quantised path, and the firmware demodulator's own error. Run it at four spot frequencies, not as a sweep.
MC Nominal corner only, castalia_pm_25, on the AC test. Whether the calibration constant is stable enough for a drift instrument — the instrument must be steadier than the drift it measures. Confirm cross-output references survive the first MC run before trusting any distribution (house rule).
PSS/PAC is not needed and should not be used. Nothing in the potentiostat chain switches: the excitation is a large-signal square only in the sense that it steps between two DC levels, and the circuit around the operating point is LTI. AC + STB gives the exact small-signal answer. PSS/PAC belongs to the island, whose choppers make it genuinely periodic-time-varying — and that difference is itself a reason to prefer Option B, since every analysis in its verification plan is one the existing benches already run.

10 · If the island stays: the bias-net fix

Should §11 Q1 come back requiring 100 kHz, the collision must be resolved before anatop_eis can be drawn. The two options in afe_rev2_eis.html §2 are not equally priced.

Rename the globals, not the pins — and rename all four. Change bnm! bnc! bpm! bpc! to eis_bnm! eis_bnc! eis_bpm! eis_bpc! throughout the copied island. Three reasons the §2 cost argument inverts:
  • Pinning touches layout; renaming does not. Twenty of the copied cells carry leon's layouts, which distribute bias as global nets. Lifting bias to boundary pins means adding pin geometry and routing to every layout that consumes bias — the expensive, error-prone half — whereas a net rename is a scriptable pass over schematic and layout net names that leaves the geometry untouched.
  • It keeps re-copy cheap. Leon is a live workspace. A rename-only delta means any future re-copy is a mechanical re-application; a pinning delta means re-doing the boundary work.
  • Rename the two that do not collide as well. Prefixing all four gives the island a uniform bias namespace, so no future AFE net can collide, and it makes the audit trivial: after the pass, no net matching ^b[np] may appear in any anatop_eis_* view.
Then re-run the 0-unbound / 0-leon-reference audit over both schematic and layout views — the 2026-08-16 finding that layout hierarchy ≠ schematic hierarchy (the IDAC and CBIA layouts instantiate AMUXTGDX16A and INVX1A, which no schematic references) applies to the rename sweep too. And note the SKILL trap already burned into castalia_eis_copy.il: dbSetInstHeaderMasterName purges the old header, so snapshot names before mutating anything.

11 · Open questions

#QuestionWhy it matters
1Is anything above ~30 kHz, or Config B bulk-tissue impedance, a product requirement — or is the requirement only the WE-interface fouling arc? This single answer decides (d) against (c). Everything below 5.6 kHz is covered at ±1 % by the existing chain; the island exists to buy the decade above, and no document in the set states a requirement for it.
2Is the shipping vref DAC the 12-bit anatop_pixel_dacR2R12 characterised in the TRM, or the 14-bit Dac2V5R2R14 named in the strategy doc and CLAUDE.md? 4× in excitation resolution (528.6 µV vs 152.6 µV delivered LSB). It also invalidates the DPV program's stated 152.6 µV quantisation if the answer is 12-bit.
3Which amplifier is A1 — anatop_tia_amp_ab (TRM: 72.24 dB into the cell) or the amp behind the "13 dB into 32 kΩ" note? If the TRM figure stands, afe_rev2_eis.html §10's V-3 warning should be retracted and the gate marked passed on existing data. If it does not, §4 and §5 of this document move.
4Does the SAR keep 20 MHz / 869.6 kS/s in the four-channel top? §10 assumed ~80 kS/s. Coherent sampling, harmonic rejection and the record-time table all rest on ~870 kS/s. At 80 kS/s the ±5 % band would be sampling-limited near 5 kHz rather than loop-limited at 28 kHz.
5Is Cf = 22 pF committed, and fixed or programmable? Every band number in §6 assumes 22 pF fixed. Fixed is the right answer for EIS: over-damping buys nothing and costs band. The 211–844 pF table in afe_rev2_strategy.html should be struck — it was built on a 6.387 MHz fu and predicted Q = 4741 against 1.79 measured.
6Excitation amplitude policy: 10 mV standard, 4 mV to reach the 100 Ω corner? 4 mV is 7.6 DAC codes. Acceptable only with the RE tap; without it the excitation is unknown at the 10 % level.
7Calibration impedance on-chip, or an external known R through the same pads? External is cheaper and calibrates the pads and board parasitics too, but needs a firmware-visible convention and a user who wires it. On-chip is one T-gate and two passives per site and works unattended — which a drift instrument in a wound dressing needs.
8Simultaneous or interleaved V and I acquisition? Interleaving through one SAR is free and exact for a stationary cell. Borrowing a neighbouring channel's idle SAR gives true simultaneity for MUX routing only. Decide before the adcSourceSel map is frozen.
